The big ideaForging: lose now, win later

You pulled four Kings. You can play them and almost surely take the pot, or you can forge them into one upgraded card. A forged card is still "in the forge" this hand, so you play with whatever's left. You'll probably lose, but not definitely.

Design rulesBorrowed from how Balatro was made

1. The theme does the teaching.

Every name is real collector lingo: foil, slab, 1st Edition, code card, misprint. Players already know what "mint" means, so we never invent jargon.

2. Numbers go up.

Every hand scores Chips × Mult. At the showdown the higher score takes the pot. Poker rank only sets the base, and your build does the rest.

3. Hook into broad things.

Collectibles care about ranks, suits, hand types, finishes, bets or deck size, never "works with Collectible X." Narrow combos almost never come up, and that feels bad.

4. Four lines, max.

If an effect needs more text, it isn't broad enough. Cut it.

5. Strong means conditional.

×3 mult needs a condition you have to build toward. Mix in boring, reliable +4 mult items so builds don't feel forced.

6. No new mechanics.

Collectibles only bend rules that already exist. Every new item makes the existing synergies richer instead of watering them down.

7. Forging is spice, not the spine.

LocalThunk's first build was Super Auto Pets-style card leveling, and it wasn't fun until jokers took over. Collectibles carry the run. Forging is a big decision you make a few times per run.

8. Seeds visible from day one.

Players shared Balatro seeds even though LocalThunk didn't care about them. Our Battle Royale mode is built on seeds, so put the seed on screen.

Layer 3 · Collectibles (display case)

Your jokers. They go in 5 slots, are scored left to right, and sell for half price.

They come from the Collector pack's rare slot, the singles case (2 random each shop, no repeats per run), and Repacks. Grails only come from Redemption inserts. Some are rolled when pulled. A Playmat gets a random suit and a PSA 10 Slab gets a random rank, so two runs never see the same case.
